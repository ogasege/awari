# Scoring the submission against the five judging criteria

Spec 26-final-review, brief section 7. Each criterion is 20%. The judges' questions are quoted from
`docs/rules-alignment.md`. "Today" is the state after fix batch 1 and the second review round; numbers say where they come from.

## 1. Potential Impact (20%)

**The judges ask:** "a credible case for solving a real problem for a real audience… Would someone actually use this?"

**Our answer today.** Yes for the audience named, with the limits stated. The audience is children of about 8 to 14 and the
parents and teachers who share one phone or one school computer where the connection is patchy or costly. The problem is
cited, not asserted: in the ITU's Facts and Figures 2024 a low-income-economy subscriber pays 19 times as much of their
income for the mobile data basket as a high-income one; Giga (ITU and UNICEF) reported in July 2024 that about three million
schools, roughly half, are still not connected; and the ITU estimates 27 per cent of people are online in low-income
countries against 93 per cent in high-income ones. Each figure is linked in the README and `docs/devpost.md`
("Who it is for and why it matters"); any figure that could not be verified was left out.

**Evidence.** One visit saves about 41 MB (1,575 files); it then works with no connection in the browser, as an installed
app and as a desktop app. Measured: `docs/final-review/slow3g.md` (usable on slow 3G in 3.4 s for Home, 5.1 s for an
article, 97 to 159 KB transferred); `offline-check` 20 steps; the offline crawl in `docs/final-review/crawl-live-offline-after.md`.

**What it is not, said in the write-up:** Simple English Wikipedia selected and checked by rules, not written by experts;
quizzes generated by rules; no sound; picture selection automatic.

**Changed in this spec:** the section above in README and devpost; a measured size; the offline claim made exact (F-001, F-016).

## 4. Innovation/Idea (20%)

**The judges ask:** "does the project differ from existing concepts? Are any unusual technologies or public resources (like datasets) leveraged or combined in a unique way?"

**Our answer today.** A fair comparison is in the README and devpost ("How it differs from what exists"): reading Wikipedia
online, offline readers such as Kiwix, and children's encyclopedia sites. Awari's difference is exploring and play (the
Castle, tours, stamp book, atlas, timeline), every quiz answer shown with the sentence that proves it, five public datasets
combined by rules (Simple English Wikipedia, Wikidata, Natural Earth, Open English WordNet, Wikimedia Commons) and every
picture licence verified against the Commons API.

**Claims checked against the code:** proof slip (`QuestionCard.tsx`), five datasets (`scripts/packs`, `scripts/media`),
licence check (`npm run licences`: 4,021 images, 0 problems after batch 1), no account (progress in IndexedDB).
Claims **not** made: that Awari is better than Kiwix as a reader, or that its content is edited by experts.

## 5. Presentation (20%)

**The judges ask:** "Does the video clearly demonstrate the project working end-to-end? Does the pitch communicate what problem is solved, who it's for, and why it matters?… creative and memorable?"

**Our answer today.** The video is the owner's to record. The script (`docs/video-script.md`) was re-checked against the
live site: every shot was exercised by a journey in the crawl (Home, search, a word card, the Nigeria quiz with hints and
proof, the Rivers tour with two next stops, the Space wing, the Oceania timeline filter, offline). The first 20 seconds now
say the problem (about half the world's schools are not online), who it is for (children of eight to fourteen) and why it
matters. The Kiro rows show the Subagents panel, the Hooks panel and a spec file. No brand names or logos are on screen and
there is no music. `docs/video-recording.md` gives the exact steps to record it in one sitting.
